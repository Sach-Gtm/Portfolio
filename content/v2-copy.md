## Positioning — read this first

v1 called you **"Software Engineer | System Architect | Growth Leader"** and led with *"I design and work on systems where performance, reliability, and scale actually matter."*

The problem: "System Architect" isn't supported by anything else on the page, and the skills list (C++ basic, Java, HTML/CSS/JS, SQL) reads as a student's. A recruiter spots that gap in about four seconds, and it makes them discount the parts that *are* genuinely strong.

What's actually strong here — and unusual:

1. **You've supported real software in the field, with real consequences.** Medical devices, in clinics, PAN-India, training doctors. 40% downtime reduction. Most students have never seen a production user, let alone stood next to one.
2. **You close things.** ₹1 lakh+ in sponsorships from TVS, Unacademy, StockGrow. 5,000+ event footfall. 40+ volunteers. Placement Cell president.
3. **You reduce time-to-resolution.** 4 days → 1 day at Darsa AI. That's a systems-thinking result, not a support result.

So v2 leads with *engineer who has shipped and supported real systems, and who can run things* — which is true, provable, and far more memorable than a generic "System Architect."
